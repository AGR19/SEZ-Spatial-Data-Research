Paper: "Can Industrial Policy Overcome Coordination Failures?"
Author: Tishara Garg, Stanford University
Context: India's industrial zones as coordination devices for shifting
         economies from low- to high-industrialization equilibria.

================================================================================
1. RESEARCH QUESTION & THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

Central question: Can government-designated industrial zones overcome
coordination failures — not just by improving physical fundamentals (roads,
land, infrastructure) but by coordinating firm expectations toward high-
industrialization equilibria?

Theoretical roots: Rosenstein-Rodan (1943) "big push" theory, extended by
Myrdal (1957) and Hirschman (1958). In settings with multiple equilibria,
government policy can coordinate private actors to select better outcomes.

Key innovation: Garg distinguishes between two channels through which
industrial zones affect outcomes:
  (a) FUNDAMENTALS channel — better infrastructure, land, connectivity
  (b) COORDINATION channel — self-fulfilling expectations that shift
      the equilibrium type (from low-industrialization to high)

She decomposes the total treatment effect into these two channels, which
is rarely done empirically.

================================================================================
2. IDENTIFICATION STRATEGY
================================================================================

Primary method: Event study Difference-in-Differences (DiD)

The strategy exploits:
  - STAGGERED ROLLOUT: Industrial zones established in different Indian
    municipalities at different times (decentralized state-level decisions)
  - TREATMENT: Binary indicator for first zone establishment in a given
    census period
  - CONTROLS: Municipalities >25km from any zone, matched via propensity
    score on baseline characteristics

Specification:
  y_it = alpha_i + beta_r(t) + sum_s beta_DiD,s * 1[TreatRound_i=s] * Zone_i + epsilon_it

Where:
  alpha_i      = municipality fixed effects
  beta_r(t)    = state x period fixed effects
  beta_DiD,s   = treatment effect s periods after zone establishment
  Clustering   = at municipality level

Critical assumption: Parallel trends — validated by showing no pre-trends
in the event study leads.

Beyond standard DiD, Garg builds a three-step STRUCTURAL estimation:
  Step 1: Recover unobserved fundamentals via GMM using zone instruments
  Step 2: Enumerate ALL possible equilibria via total-degree homotopy
          continuation (algebraic geometry technique)
  Step 3: Decompose treatment effects into fundamentals vs coordination

3. DATA CONSTRUCTION — How She Built the Dataset
================================================================================

This is the most directly useful section for our Philippines work.

ZONE DATA:
  - Source: India Industrial Land Bank + RTI (Right to Information) requests
    to State Industrial Development Corporations
  - Coverage: ~4,000 geocoded industrial zones
  - Establishment dates: Available for 1,585 zones
  - Exclusions: Removed SEZs and Export Promotion Zones to focus on
    standard industrial parks (analogous to our manufacturing zones)
  - Geocoding: Polygon centroids used as zone locations

OUTCOME DATA:
  | Dataset               | Source                 | Spatial Unit   | Time Periods        |
  |-----------------------|------------------------|----------------|---------------------|
  | Economic Census       | Ministry of Statistics  | Municipality   | 1990, 1998, 2005, 2013 |
  | Population Census     | Government of India    | Municipality   | 1991, 2001, 2011    |
  | Night lights          | DMSP-OLS               | 1/120 deg grid | 1994-2013           |
  | Company registrations | Min. Corporate Affairs | ZIP code       | Various             |
  | Historical roads      | Digitized from archives| Network layers | Multiple periods    |

SPATIAL CONSTRUCTION:
  - Matched municipalities to zones using geocoded coordinates
  - Distance buffers: 25km (main), 10-15km (spillover analysis)
  - Catchment areas: ~627 labor markets partitioned into inner rings
    and peripheral hinterland
  - Historical roads: DIGITIZED from archival maps — a major data
    construction effort to control for concurrent infrastructure changes

KEY LESSON: She did not rely on a single downloadable dataset. She
assembled the zone list from government records + RTI requests, geocoded
them, and merged with census data. The data construction IS the research.

4. SPATIAL METHODS
================================================================================

COORDINATE SYSTEM:
  - Geodetic coordinates (latitude/longitude) for storage
  - Great-circle (haversine) distances between zone centroids and
    municipality centers

DISTANCE CALCULATIONS:
  - Distance bins: 5km increments (0-5, 5-10, 10-15, 15-20, 20-25km)
  - Spillover decay: Estimated as exp(-eta * d) with eta = 0.71
    meaning spillovers halve every ~1km
  - Control group: Municipalities >25km from any zone

SPATIAL JOIN APPROACH:
  - Municipality boundaries mapped to zones via buffer radius
  - Treatment assignment: (i) zone established in census period,
    (ii) no prior zone within 25km

SPILLOVER SPECIFICATION:
  y_imt = alpha_i + gamma + sum_b beta_DiD,b * 1[b(i)=b] * Zone_m + X_it + epsilon_imt
  Distance bins b: 0-5km, 5-10km, 10-15km, 15-20km, 20-25km

IMPORTANT: Her 5km bin structure is specific to India's density. For the
Philippines (lower population density, island geography), different bin
widths would be appropriate. Do NOT replicate her exact bin structure.

5. HOW SHE HANDLES DATA LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

This is critical for the assignment's evaluation criteria — transparency.

  1. MISSING ESTABLISHMENT DATES: Only 1,585 of 4,000 zones have dates.
     She acknowledges this and works with the subset, explaining the
     coverage implications.

  2. SUTVA CONCERNS: If spillovers contaminate controls, estimates are
     biased. She addresses this by:
     - Using distance-restricted controls (>25km)
     - Testing robustness with tighter restrictions (10km, 15km, 20km)
     - Showing estimates DECLINE with tighter restrictions (consistent
       with positive spillovers, not contamination)

  3. MULTIPLE EQUILIBRIA IDENTIFICATION: She explicitly states where her
     assumptions differ from prior theoretical literature and what could
     violate them.

  4. INFRASTRUCTURE CONFOUNDING: Road construction often accompanies zone
     establishment. She addresses this by digitizing historical road
     networks and controlling for road proximity as time-varying covariate.

Pattern: She never hides a limitation. She states it, explains its
implication for estimates, and shows robustness checks that bound the
concern. This is exactly what the assignment evaluators want to see.

6. KEY FINDINGS & MAGNITUDES
================================================================================

  - 60% increase in industrial employment within 15 years
  - 125-142 more non-farm jobs per km^2 (hired workers)
  - 37-41 more firms per km^2
  - Positive spillovers extend 10-15km from zone
  - Service sector spillovers to non-targeted sectors (agglomeration evidence)
  - Treatment effects are BIMODAL — some municipalities switch equilibria,
    others see only marginal improvement
  - 38% increase in likelihood of transitioning from low to high
    industrialization equilibrium
  - 32.47% of total effect attributable to coordination (vs 67.53% fundamentals)
  - Structural estimate: Adding 100 non-farm workers increases local
    productivity by 3.14% (delta = 61.94)

7. METHODOLOGICAL LESSONS FOR PHILIPPINES ADAPTATION
================================================================================

(These are lessons about HOW to do research, not specifications to copy)

  a) DATA ASSEMBLY IS THE RESEARCH: Don't expect a clean downloadable
     dataset. The value is in carefully constructing zone lists from
     government records, geocoding them, and documenting every step.

  b) TRANSPARENCY OVER COMPLETENESS: The assignment explicitly values
     honest documentation of limitations over polished-looking results.

  c) DISTANCE MATTERS: Choosing the right spatial unit and distance
     thresholds requires understanding local geography. The Philippines
     is an archipelago — distances across water are not the same as
     overland distances. This is a real methodological consideration.

  d) STAGGERED TIMING ENABLES CAUSAL INFERENCE: If zones were established
     at different times, DiD becomes feasible. Identifying establishment
     years is therefore a high-priority data task.

  e) MULTIPLE OUTCOME SOURCES STRENGTHEN RESULTS: Night lights, census
     employment, firm registrations — triangulating across independent
     data sources is how she validates findings.

  f) CONTROL GROUP CONSTRUCTION: The control group is not "all other
     municipalities" — it is carefully restricted to comparable units
     that are far enough from zones to avoid spillover contamination.

  g) PROPENSITY SCORE MATCHING: Matching treated and control units on
     baseline characteristics (road proximity, distance to towns, water
     sources) strengthens the parallel trends assumption.
