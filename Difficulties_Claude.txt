0. ACADEMIC & SCHOLARLY DATASET SEARCH — No Pre-Built Philippines Dataset Found
================================================================================

A systematic search of Google Scholar, data repositories (Harvard Dataverse,
Zenodo, Figshare, World Bank Data Catalog), and cross-country SEZ databases
was conducted to determine whether any researcher has already constructed
a geocoded dataset of Philippine industrial zones or economic zones.

SEARCHES CONDUCTED:
  - Google Scholar: "Philippines special economic zones spatial dataset GIS"
  - Google Scholar: "industrial zones" OR "economic zones" Philippines dataset geocoded
  - Google Scholar: "PEZA Philippines firm location employment spatial"
  - Google Scholar: "industrial parks" OR "industrial estates" Philippines dataset coordinates
  - Google Scholar: "place-based industrial policy" geocoded database developing countries
  - Google Scholar: "night lights" Philippines municipalities economic zones
  - Harvard Dataverse: "special economic zones Philippines"
  - Zenodo: "special economic zones Philippines" (type=dataset)
  - Figshare: "special economic zones"
  - World Bank Data Catalog: "special economic zones"

KEY FINDINGS:

(a) NO pre-built geocoded dataset of Philippine industrial zones exists
    in any public repository searched. This is a genuine data gap.

(b) CROSS-COUNTRY SEZ DATABASES exist but have limitations:

    - Frick, Rodriguez-Pose & Wong (2019, Economic Geography): Assembled
      346 geocoded SEZs across 22 emerging countries. Paper accessible
      via Taylor & Francis (DOI: 10.1080/00130095.2018.1462774). Could
      NOT verify if Philippines is included — full paper behind paywall.
      Replication data availability: UNKNOWN (403 on SSRN, T&F blocked).

    - World Bank (2017) "Special Economic Zones: An Operational Review":
      Database covering 553 SEZs across 51 countries. Philippines coverage
      NOT confirmed. Data download attempted at World Bank S3 bucket —
      returned 403 (Forbidden).

    - UNCTAD World Investment Report 2019, Chapter 4: Reportedly mapped
      ~5,400 SEZs across 147 economies. Philippines LIKELY included given
      scope. However, UNCTAD PDF returned 403, and the underlying database
      does not appear to be publicly downloadable as a structured file.

(c) PAPERS USING PHILIPPINE SPATIAL DATA (but no downloadable zone datasets):

    - Sanders & Brown (2012): "Migratory response of labor to SEZs in the
      Philippines, 1995-2005." Used GIS techniques and "geo-spatial location
      of SEZ" but no replication data found publicly.

    - Ortega, Acielo & Hermida (2015): "Mega-regions in the Philippines:
      Accounting for special economic zones." Analyzed spatial patterns of
      SEZs but no downloadable dataset.

    - Kleibert (2018): "Exclusive development(s): Special economic zones
      and enclave urbanism in the Philippines." Qualitative spatial analysis.

    - Habana & Choi (2024): "Impact of SEZs on intra-regional income
      inequality in the Philippines." Used PEZA performance data 2018-2022
      but no geocoded zone dataset.

    - Garces, Jandoc & Lu (2021): "Political dynasties and economic
      development: Evidence using nighttime light in the Philippines."
      Built a panel of Philippine municipalities with night lights — but
      replication data not indicated as publicly available.

    - Ramel, Legaspi & Pajaron (2026): "Illuminating the land: effects of
      nighttime lights on land values in the Philippines." Uses night lights
      at municipality level — potential methodological reference.

(d) INDUSTRIAL PARKS SPECIFICALLY:

    - Garcia, Peralta et al. (2003): Used MS Access database with firm
      coordinates in Luzon industrial estates — NOT publicly available,
      and data is >20 years old.

    - Aggarwal (2022): Consolidated data on BIMP-EAGA industrial estates
      (Brunei-Indonesia-Malaysia-Philippines) — availability unclear.

    - Cauba & Coniato (2021): GIS-based site selection for economic zones
      in Butuan City — local study, no national dataset.

CONCLUSION:
  No researcher has published a ready-to-use geocoded dataset of Philippine
  industrial zones. The PEZA Excel files we downloaded (1.xls with 74
  manufacturing zones, 3.xls with 22+6 agro-industrial zones) represent
  the best available structured data. Geocoding these zone locations from
  their textual addresses to coordinates is original work that adds value.

  The cross-country databases (Frick et al., World Bank, UNCTAD) may
  contain Philippine entries but their underlying data is not freely
  downloadable. These should be cited as "sources seriously considered"
  in sources.csv with a note about access barriers.

  This finding reinforces the assignment's premise: constructing the
  zone dataset IS the hard, original contribution.

1. PEZA MANUFACTURING ZONE LIST — FOUND (Excel download from PEZA)
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  A comprehensive, downloadable list of all 74 PEZA-registered manufacturing
  economic zones with zone names, GPS coordinates, municipality, province,
  area (hectares), establishment year, and sector/activity type.

What was found:
  *** RESOLVED *** An official PEZA Excel file was located at:
    https://www.peza.gov.ph/sites/default/files/1.xls

  This file contains the complete "LIST OF OPERATING MANUFACTURING ECONOMIC
  ZONES" as of 30 November 2017. It lists all 74 zones with:
    - Zone number (1-74)
    - Zone name
    - Location (barangay, municipality/city, province)
    - Region
    - Developer/operator name
    - Land area (hectares)
    - Approved investments (PHP millions)
    - Corporate nationality (ownership breakdown)

  The file has been downloaded locally as: peza_zones_raw.xls (55 KB)

  NOTE: The PEZA website has an expired SSL certificate, so the download
  requires bypassing certificate verification (curl -k flag). The file
  itself is a valid Composite Document (Excel .xls), last saved Feb 2018.

  Geographic concentration of zones:
    - Laguna: 14 zones (heaviest concentration)
    - Cavite: 11 zones
    - Batangas: 10 zones
    - Pampanga: 6 zones
    - NCR: 6 zones
    - Cebu: 7 zones
    (CALABARZON corridor south of Manila dominates)

  Largest zones by area:
    - Clark Special Economic Zone: 29,365 hectares
    - Phividec Industrial Estate: 3,000 hectares
    - Taganito SEZ: 687.5 hectares
    - Lima Technology Center: 430 hectares
    - Leyte Industrial Development Estate: 425 hectares
    - Rio Tuba Export Processing Zone: 424 hectares

Remaining limitations:
  - No GPS coordinates or polygon boundaries — locations are textual
    (barangay + municipality + province). Geocoding to municipality
    centroids is needed.
  - Data is as of November 2017 — zones proclaimed after this date
    are not included.
  - No establishment year column — dates must be sourced separately.
  - No sector/activity type column — all are classified as
    "manufacturing" but actual industrial activities vary.

Status: COMPLETE LIST AVAILABLE — 74 manufacturing zones with names,
locations, area, and developer. Geocoding required for spatial analysis.

2. PEZA ZONE COORDINATES — Not Publicly Available in Structured Format
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Latitude/longitude coordinates or polygon boundaries for individual
  industrial zones.

What was found:
  - PEZA does not publish a GIS layer, shapefile, or coordinate file.
  - The "Economic Zone Locator" on peza.gov.ph may contain a map interface
    but is JavaScript-rendered and not scrapable.
  - No Philippine government GIS portal was found with zone boundaries.
  - NAMRIA (National Mapping and Resource Information Authority) is the
    official mapping agency but its data catalog was not publicly browsable.

Why this matters:
  Without coordinates, zones cannot be placed on a map or linked to
  spatial datasets (night lights, population density, etc.).

Workarounds:
  a) Geocode zone names using municipality/city location as proxy.
     Most zones are identified by their municipality (e.g., "Cavite
     Economic Zone" in Rosario, Cavite). Municipality centroids from
     GADM or the Philippines JSON maps repo can serve as approximate
     zone locations.
  b) For major zones (Subic, Clark, Mactan, Cavite), Google Maps satellite
     imagery shows clear industrial park boundaries that could be manually
     digitized.
  c) OpenStreetMap may have some industrial zone boundaries tagged as
     landuse=industrial — worth checking in the Philippines .osm.pbf extract.

Status: NOT DIRECTLY AVAILABLE — requires geocoding or manual construction.

3. PHILIPPINE STATISTICS AUTHORITY (PSA) — Portal Access Issues
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Municipality-level economic data: employment by sector, number of
  establishments, GDP/GRP, census population counts.

What was found:
  - psa.gov.ph is the main portal but returned HTTP 403 (Forbidden) when
    accessed programmatically.
  - openstat.psa.gov.ph (the open data portal) also returned 403.
  - PSA publishes the Census of Population and Housing, Annual Survey of
    Philippine Business and Industry (ASPBI), and Census of Philippine
    Business and Industry — but these appear to be available only through
    their web interface or by request, not as bulk downloads.

Why this matters:
  Municipality-level economic data is essential for comparing zone vs
  non-zone locations (Part 3 of the assignment) and for constructing
  the analysis_units.csv file.

Workarounds:
  a) World Bank Philippines data portal has country-level indicators in
     CSV/Excel format — useful but NOT subnational.
  b) Philippine Statistical Yearbooks may contain provincial/regional data
     in PDF format that could be extracted.
  c) HDX (Humanitarian Data Exchange) at data.humdata.org sometimes hosts
     Philippines subnational datasets but returned 403 on attempted access.
  d) The PhilAtlas website (philatlas.com) has municipality profiles that
     include census population — could be scraped if needed.

Status: COUNTRY-LEVEL DATA AVAILABLE; MUNICIPALITY-LEVEL REQUIRES
ALTERNATIVE SOURCES OR DIRECT PSA REQUEST.

4. LAND VALUES — Very Limited Public Availability
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Land prices or zonal land values at the municipality level for the
  Philippines. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) sets "zonal values"
  for tax purposes, which would be the closest proxy.

What was found:
  - BIR zonal values exist but are published per Revenue District Office,
    often in PDF gazette format, not as a consolidated spatial dataset.
  - No centralized downloadable file of land values per municipality.
  - Private real estate platforms (Lamudi, Property24) have asking prices
    but these are not suitable for academic research.

Why this matters:
  Land values are one of the requested comparison variables in the
  assignment. They are also theoretically important — Garg's research
  emphasizes how land pricing variation affects firm entry.

Workarounds:
  a) Use night lights as a proxy for economic activity/land value
     (established correlation in development economics literature).
  b) Built-up area from GHSL could serve as urbanization proxy.
  c) If time permits, a sample of BIR zonal values could be manually
     extracted from gazette PDFs for major municipalities.

Status: NOT AVAILABLE AS STRUCTURED DATA — requires manual extraction
or proxy variables.

5. ZONE ESTABLISHMENT YEARS — Scattered Across Multiple Sources
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Year of establishment/proclamation for each industrial zone, needed for
  Part 4 of the assignment (dynamic/causal extension).

What was found:
  - Presidential Proclamations designating zones are published individually
    but not compiled in a single list.
  - Some establishment dates can be inferred from Wikipedia entries,
    news articles, and zone websites.
  - RA 7916 (1995 Special Economic Zone Act) provides the legal framework
    but individual zone dates vary widely (1972 for Bataan EPZ through
    2020s for newer zones).

Workarounds:
  a) Cross-reference Wikipedia zone listings with Presidential Proclamation
     numbers (which contain dates).
  b) PEZA annual reports may contain lists of newly proclaimed zones by year.
  c) News article archives (BusinessWorld, Rappler, Inquirer) often report
     zone proclamations.

Status: PARTIALLY AVAILABLE — requires compilation from multiple sources.

9. SUMMARY OF FEASIBILITY ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

FEASIBLE with available data:
  - Constructing a PARTIAL zone dataset from Wikipedia + PEZA web scraping
  - Geocoding zones to municipality level using admin boundary centroids
  - Merging with population density (WorldPop), night lights (VIIRS),
    built-up area (GHSL), distances to infrastructure (OSM)
  - Comparing zone vs non-zone municipalities on these variables

REQUIRES ADDITIONAL EFFORT (beyond current session):
  - Complete manufacturing zone list (FOI request to PEZA)
  - Precise zone coordinates/boundaries (manual digitization or OSM extraction)
  - Municipality-level economic data (PSA portal access or manual extraction)
  - Land values (BIR gazette PDF extraction)
  - Zone establishment years (multi-source compilation)
