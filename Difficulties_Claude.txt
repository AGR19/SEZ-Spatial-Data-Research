DIFFICULTIES ENCOUNTERED IN DATASET RESEARCH — Philippines Industrial Zones
================================================================================

Document created: 2026-05-03
Purpose: Honest documentation of data access challenges encountered while
researching datasets for the Philippines industrial zones spatial analysis.

================================================================================
1. PEZA MANUFACTURING ZONE LIST — No Downloadable Structured Dataset
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  A comprehensive, downloadable list of all 74 PEZA-registered manufacturing
  economic zones with zone names, GPS coordinates, municipality, province,
  area (hectares), establishment year, and sector/activity type.

What was found:
  - PEZA website (peza.gov.ph) is operational (HTTP 200) but has an expired
    SSL certificate, causing most automated tools to reject the connection.
  - The "Operating Economic Zones" page exists at:
    https://www.peza.gov.ph/operating-economic-zones
  - However, zone listings are loaded via JavaScript (Drupal CMS) — the page
    renders zone data dynamically, making automated scraping unreliable.
  - The "Economic Zone Locator" page exists but appears to be an interactive
    tool, not a downloadable dataset.
  - No CSV, Excel, or shapefile download found anywhere on the PEZA site.
  - PEZA has a "Downloads" section at /downloads, but it contains regulatory
    documents (issuances, forms), not data files.

Why this matters:
  Manufacturing zones are the PRIMARY dataset for this assignment. Without a
  downloadable structured list, the zone dataset must be constructed manually
  from the PEZA website, Wikipedia compilations, and news articles.

Workarounds:
  a) Wikipedia's "List of economic zones in the Philippines" provides partial
     data for agro-industrial zones (22 zones with names, locations, area,
     operators) and free ports (11 zones). Manufacturing zone details are
     mentioned as a category (74 zones) but NOT individually listed.
  b) PEZA's Freedom of Information (FOI) request portal exists at:
     https://www.peza.gov.ph/foi-request — this could be used to formally
     request the zone master list with coordinates.
  c) Manual geocoding: Using zone names + municipality names from whatever
     partial list can be assembled, geocode via Nominatim/Google Maps API.
  d) Academic sources: Some research papers (Sanders & Brown 2012, Ortega
     et al. 2015) may include zone lists in their appendices.

Status: PARTIAL DATA AVAILABLE — full manufacturing zone list requires
manual assembly or FOI request.

2. PEZA ZONE COORDINATES — Not Publicly Available in Structured Format
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Latitude/longitude coordinates or polygon boundaries for individual
  industrial zones.

What was found:
  - PEZA does not publish a GIS layer, shapefile, or coordinate file.
  - The "Economic Zone Locator" on peza.gov.ph may contain a map interface
    but is JavaScript-rendered and not scrapable.
  - No Philippine government GIS portal was found with zone boundaries.
  - NAMRIA (National Mapping and Resource Information Authority) is the
    official mapping agency but its data catalog was not publicly browsable.

Why this matters:
  Without coordinates, zones cannot be placed on a map or linked to
  spatial datasets (night lights, population density, etc.).

Workarounds:
  a) Geocode zone names using municipality/city location as proxy.
     Most zones are identified by their municipality (e.g., "Cavite
     Economic Zone" in Rosario, Cavite). Municipality centroids from
     GADM or the Philippines JSON maps repo can serve as approximate
     zone locations.
  b) For major zones (Subic, Clark, Mactan, Cavite), Google Maps satellite
     imagery shows clear industrial park boundaries that could be manually
     digitized.
  c) OpenStreetMap may have some industrial zone boundaries tagged as
     landuse=industrial — worth checking in the Philippines .osm.pbf extract.

Status: NOT DIRECTLY AVAILABLE — requires geocoding or manual construction.

3. PHILIPPINE STATISTICS AUTHORITY (PSA) — Portal Access Issues
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Municipality-level economic data: employment by sector, number of
  establishments, GDP/GRP, census population counts.

What was found:
  - psa.gov.ph is the main portal but returned HTTP 403 (Forbidden) when
    accessed programmatically.
  - openstat.psa.gov.ph (the open data portal) also returned 403.
  - PSA publishes the Census of Population and Housing, Annual Survey of
    Philippine Business and Industry (ASPBI), and Census of Philippine
    Business and Industry — but these appear to be available only through
    their web interface or by request, not as bulk downloads.

Why this matters:
  Municipality-level economic data is essential for comparing zone vs
  non-zone locations (Part 3 of the assignment) and for constructing
  the analysis_units.csv file.

Workarounds:
  a) World Bank Philippines data portal has country-level indicators in
     CSV/Excel format — useful but NOT subnational.
  b) Philippine Statistical Yearbooks may contain provincial/regional data
     in PDF format that could be extracted.
  c) HDX (Humanitarian Data Exchange) at data.humdata.org sometimes hosts
     Philippines subnational datasets but returned 403 on attempted access.
  d) The PhilAtlas website (philatlas.com) has municipality profiles that
     include census population — could be scraped if needed.

Status: COUNTRY-LEVEL DATA AVAILABLE; MUNICIPALITY-LEVEL REQUIRES
ALTERNATIVE SOURCES OR DIRECT PSA REQUEST.

4. LAND VALUES — Very Limited Public Availability
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Land prices or zonal land values at the municipality level for the
  Philippines. The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) sets "zonal values"
  for tax purposes, which would be the closest proxy.

What was found:
  - BIR zonal values exist but are published per Revenue District Office,
    often in PDF gazette format, not as a consolidated spatial dataset.
  - No centralized downloadable file of land values per municipality.
  - Private real estate platforms (Lamudi, Property24) have asking prices
    but these are not suitable for academic research.

Why this matters:
  Land values are one of the requested comparison variables in the
  assignment. They are also theoretically important — Garg's research
  emphasizes how land pricing variation affects firm entry.

Workarounds:
  a) Use night lights as a proxy for economic activity/land value
     (established correlation in development economics literature).
  b) Built-up area from GHSL could serve as urbanization proxy.
  c) If time permits, a sample of BIR zonal values could be manually
     extracted from gazette PDFs for major municipalities.

Status: NOT AVAILABLE AS STRUCTURED DATA — requires manual extraction
or proxy variables.

================================================================================
5. ZONE ESTABLISHMENT YEARS — Scattered Across Multiple Sources
================================================================================

What was searched for:
  Year of establishment/proclamation for each industrial zone, needed for
  Part 4 of the assignment (dynamic/causal extension).

What was found:
  - Presidential Proclamations designating zones are published individually
    but not compiled in a single list.
  - Some establishment dates can be inferred from Wikipedia entries,
    news articles, and zone websites.
  - RA 7916 (1995 Special Economic Zone Act) provides the legal framework
    but individual zone dates vary widely (1972 for Bataan EPZ through
    2020s for newer zones).

Workarounds:
  a) Cross-reference Wikipedia zone listings with Presidential Proclamation
     numbers (which contain dates).
  b) PEZA annual reports may contain lists of newly proclaimed zones by year.
  c) News article archives (BusinessWorld, Rappler, Inquirer) often report
     zone proclamations.

Status: PARTIALLY AVAILABLE — requires compilation from multiple sources.

7. SUMMARY OF FEASIBILITY ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

FEASIBLE with available data:
  - Constructing a PARTIAL zone dataset from Wikipedia + PEZA web scraping
  - Geocoding zones to municipality level using admin boundary centroids
  - Merging with population density (WorldPop), night lights (VIIRS),
    built-up area (GHSL), distances to infrastructure (OSM)
  - Comparing zone vs non-zone municipalities on these variables

REQUIRES ADDITIONAL EFFORT (beyond current session):
  - Complete manufacturing zone list (FOI request to PEZA)
  - Precise zone coordinates/boundaries (manual digitization or OSM extraction)
  - Municipality-level economic data (PSA portal access or manual extraction)
  - Land values (BIR gazette PDF extraction)
  - Zone establishment years (multi-source compilation)

The assignment explicitly states that a careful partial solution with
clear documentation of constraints is preferred over an opaque complete
solution. This difficulties document serves that purpose.
